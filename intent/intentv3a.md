Let’s Go! — Progressive Intent V3a

New Name and Proof Before Resume on the Front Page

Outcome

Two changes, both about what people see first.

1. The product is now called “Let’s Go!”, with the exclamation mark, not “Let’s Code”.
2. The front page says what it is for: Proof Before Resume. You don’t arrive with a résumé. You join a team, build something in the competition, and what you built is your proof.

Give the front page a custom image of a team building together in the AI age, and make it feel cool.

Required Outcomes

The Name

Everywhere a person sees the product name, it reads “Let’s Go!”: the header, the browser tab, the footer, and any wording on the screens.

The Message

“Proof Before Resume” appears prominently on the front page as the idea behind the whole competition, with these points in a few plain words:

no résumé is needed to take part;
you join a team and build something real;
winning teams earn recognition they can point to.

The Image

An original image made for Let’s Go!: a team of four working together, with a clear sense of the AI age. It reads well on phone and desktop, in light and dark.

Boundaries

Keep this small: the name, and the front page.

No change to team, invitation, registration, event, account, or privacy behaviour. All existing tests keep passing.

The repository, folder, and saved data keep their current names. What people see changes, and so do the README and other current documents. Earlier intent files stay as they were written.

Recognition is described, not built. No winners page, badges, rankings, or judging.

Promise only what exists. No guaranteed jobs or earnings, no invented numbers, testimonials, or sponsors. The “illustrative, not funded” prize label stays.

The image is ours: no real people’s likenesses, no stock photos, no borrowed brands. It has a text description, and it must not slow the page or push the event details and “Join free” out of reach.

The coding agent owns the wording beyond the approved phrases, the image’s style, and how it is made.

Success and Evidence

Mark opens the front page, logged out, and without scrolling far:

sees the name “Let’s Go!” and no “Let’s Code” anywhere on the screens;
sees “Proof Before Resume” and understands no résumé is needed;
sees the custom team image and finds it fits;
can still read the event details and press “Join free”.

The same holds on a phone and in dark appearance. All existing automated tests pass.

Bring the running page back for Mark to judge the look before anything else is added.
